What the study found

  • The base shape decides most of it. The custom spline (M01–M04) and the rose with an 8θ term (M07) reach fit cost 73–95. Every plain-rose model stays at 160–176, whatever its radius or twist curve. The drawing wants fuller lobes with sharper creases than a rose; a superformula (M08) can't do better than the rose.
  • The lamp in the drawing is rotated about −1.4° against twist(0) = 0. Freeing that orientation halved the cost of M01 and M04.
  • Twist is the most sensitive parameter. Scaling it by 3 % adds 0.5–0.9 mm of rib error in every model. Radius ±1 mm drops the front outline to 0/19.
  • Triangles mostly shift the outline. Height moves it 1:1, count by up to ±0.5 mm, width by ≤ 0.2 mm; none of them moves the ribs. Sections matter little: ≥ 15 sections stay within 0.15 mm of the exact curves.
  • Local control isn't the bottleneck. The 10 mm knot models move single segments cleanly (locality 0.96) but don't beat the 15–19-parameter custom-spline models.
  • Still failing in every top model: S01 (the drawn rim edge is a rounded corner) and parts of the waist S08–S11, where neighbouring ribs are off in opposite directions.
